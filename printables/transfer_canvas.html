Field / lab

Pick a task from your own research where an LLM decides whether some evidence satisfies some rule. Sketch how you would know when to trust its verdict. Be concrete: names of datasets, numbers, layers.

1Your LLM-judged task

What is judged?  What are the rules (criteria, rubric, policy)?  What is the evidence (documents, code, transcripts)?  Which judge model?

2Ground truth

Where do correct labels come from (experts, past audits, benchmark)? How many labelled cases can you get? Inter-rater agreement?

3Confidence signal

Why this one? What does it cost per verdict? Which token position do you read?

4Threshold policy

Target error among auto-accepted verdicts: %   Expected coverage (share not sent to a human): %   How will you pick the threshold (held-out set, conformal)?

5Grounding check

Which intervention shows the verdict depends on the right evidence? (remove / patch / contradict a sentence, swap a rule clause). What result would worry you?

6Distribution-shift test

What is new in deployment (new rules, new clients, new document style, new language)? Which slice do you hold out to test that the signal transfers?

7Risks and limits

When could a well-calibrated score still mislead you? Who is hurt by an escape (a wrong verdict that skips review)?
