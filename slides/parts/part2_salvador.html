Block 2 · Intervene · the metric

We score each patch by how far it restores the clean answer

LD = logit(IO) − logit(S)

IO = indirect object, the right answer (Mary) · S = subject, the repeated name (John)

restored = (LDpatched − LDcorr) / (LDclean − LDcorr)

Computed on GPT-2 small (TransformerLens), mean over NB2's 8 prompts: LDclean = +3.02, LDcorr = −4.61.

0:37–0:39. Why a logit difference and not accuracy: it is continuous and it cancels everything the two names share. Normalizing gives 0 = behaves like the corrupted run, 1 = fully restored to clean. Values above 1 or below 0 are possible; we will see a few. These are the real numbers from NB2's 8-prompt batch, so they will match the notebook.

Block 2 · Intervene · the IOI circuit

Indirect object identification is the canonical worked circuit

Wang et al. 2022, “Interpretability in the Wild”, GPT-2 small. Simplified: the paper also reports backup and negative name movers.

0:39–0:41. A teaser, not a lecture. Three groups of heads, earliest first. (1) Duplicate-token heads at the second "John" attend back to the first John and notice the repeat. Induction heads do a related job (they attend to the token after the first John); the arc omits them for clarity, but you will meet two of them (L5H5, L6H9) in the head ranking. (2) S-inhibition heads (L7H3, L7H9, L8H6, L8H10) carry "John is the duplicate, do not say John" to the final position. (3) Name mover heads (L9H9, L9H6, L10H0) at the final position attend to the remaining name, Mary, and copy it to the output. You will try to find the name movers yourselves in the notebook.

Head patching finds S-inhibition and name-mover heads, and two that fight back

Block 3 · Decompose · superposition

Sparse features can share dimensions

Illustrative toy geometry after Elhage et al. 2022, “Toy Models of Superposition”.

1:13–1:16. Toy model (Elhage et al. 2022): 5 features, a 2-dimensional hidden space, then reconstruct. Step 1: pack 5 directions into 2D. Activate feature 1 and read every direction: the others pick up part of it. That leak is interference. Step 2: if features are dense (often on together), leaks pile up on every input, so the model keeps 2 orthogonal features and drops 3. Step 3: as features become sparse, they are rarely on together; the leak is mostly cut by the ReLU, so the model packs all 5 as a pentagon. Step 4: more features than dimensions, paid for with interference. One honest clause: superposition makes individual neurons polysemantic only when the neuron basis is privileged, i.e. a nonlinearity acts per neuron, as in an MLP. This toy hidden layer is linear, so here it shows features sharing directions, not neurons as such.

Block 3 · Decompose · sparse autoencoders

An SAE is a learned dictionary of directions

JumpReLU = a ReLU with a learned threshold per latent (what Gemma Scope 2 uses). Latent labels and values illustrative.

1:16–1:19. Step 0: one residual vector, dense, every entry a little bit on. Step 1: the encoder maps it to a much wider vector where only a few latents are non-zero (sparsity penalty). Gemma Scope 2 uses JumpReLU: a ReLU with a learned threshold, so a latent is exactly 0 unless its pre-activation clears the threshold. Students will see 'jumprelu' in the NB3 config. Each latent can get a human label from the texts that activate it. Labels here are illustrative. Step 2: the decoder adds those latents' directions back up (W_dec·f + b_dec, i.e. Σ latent × direction plus a bias): a reconstruction. Step 3: the reconstruction is imperfect. The leftover, the error term, is real computation the dictionary does not explain. Concrete number from NB3: splicing the SAE reconstruction back into Gemma 3 1B IT (layer 17) raises next-token loss by +0.37 nats. Block 4 will draw this leftover as error nodes.

SAE probes are not reliably better than plain probes

113

datasets: SAE probes won on some, but not consistently, and did not beat strong baselines on average (Kantamneni et al., ICML 2025).

Mar 2025

Google DeepMind deprioritized fundamental SAE research after SAEs underperformed linear probes on out-of-distribution harmful-intent detection.

Your own race may go either way: one 200-sentence dataset is an anecdote. Benchmark against a plain probe before trusting a feature.

Block 3 · Decompose · a caution on steering

A steering effect does not identify the representation

In their experiments, directions orthogonal to a steering vector reached 95–100% of its effect.

So "steering with feature f changes the output" shows f is sufficient to cause the change, not that f is the variable the model uses.

Venkatesh & Kurapath, “On the Non-Identifiability of Steering Vectors in LLMs”, ICLR 2026 workshop (Re-Align), arXiv 2602.06801.

1:38–1:40. Source: Venkatesh & Kurapath, a poster at the ICLR 2026 Representational Alignment workshop, not the main conference. The 95–100% figure is from the paper body (arXiv v3, contributions: "orthogonal perturbations achieving 95–100% of the original steering efficacy across multiple models and traits"); the abstract says "near-equivalent efficacy". Many directions do the same job. Steering is an existence proof of an effect, not evidence of a unique representation. Combine with necessity tests (ablation, patching). This sets up Block 4: we want to validate whole explanations by intervention.

Block 4 · Trace · method

A graph comes from a replacement model, one prompt at a time

1 · Replace

Swap the MLPs for per-layer or cross-layer transcoders: sparse, interpretable features. Keep an error node for what they miss.

2 · Attribute

On one prompt, compute the direct linear effect between features, tokens, error nodes and logits.

3 · Prune

Keep the nodes and edges that carry most of the effect on the chosen logit.

Attention patterns are frozen from the real run, so the effects are linear and exact for that prompt.

1:45–1:47. A transcoder reads the MLP input and predicts the MLP output through a sparse layer. Freezing attention patterns and normalization makes the replacement linear in the features, so edge weights are exact direct effects for this one prompt. Pruning keeps a readable subgraph, often under a hundred nodes.

Block 4 · Trace · validation

A graph is a hypothesis until an intervention confirms it

Measured in NB4 (Gemma 2 2B, featured Dallas graph, Neuronpedia steering). The same two tests appear in “On the Biology of a Large Language Model” (2025).
