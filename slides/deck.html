0:09:45–0:11 (1 min 15 s). Real numbers, GPT-2 small, prompt “The Eiffel Tower is in the French city of”.

at the last position the model has one final vector x (after the last LayerNorm). It compares x with every word in its vocabulary by a dot product: one score per token, called a logit. (In GPT-2 the unembedding vectors are the embedding rows again: tied weights.)

logits are any real number; about half of the 50,257 are negative (“·banana”: −1.34). Top: ·Paris 18.06, ·Nice 17.68, ·Lyon 17.29, ·Marse (first piece of “Marseille”) 16.80, ·L 15.91, ·Saint 15.80; ·London is only rank 176.

softmax = exponentiate each logit, divide by the total. Now they are probabilities: Paris 0.207, Nice 0.141, Lyon 0.096 … and the other 50,249 tokens together still hold 0.45. Exponentiating means only differences between logits matter: Hugging Face reports these same logits shifted by −116.4 (TransformerLens centres them), and the probabilities are identical.

so we compare two candidates directly: the logit difference, here 0.77 = ln(0.207/0.096). It is a linear function of x: add a vector v to x and LD moves by exactly v · (uParis − uLyon). A probability depends on all 50,257 logits at once, so it is not. That is why Salvador's patching metric in Block 2 is a logit difference (Mary vs John). Fine print if asked: x is taken after the final LayerNorm, whose rescaling is the one non-linear step between the residual stream and the logits.

Block 3 · Decompose · superposition

Sparse features can share dimensions

Illustrative toy geometry after Elhage et al. 2022, “Toy Models of Superposition”.

1:13–1:16. Toy model (Elhage et al. 2022): 5 features, a 2-dimensional hidden space, then reconstruct.

pack 5 directions into 2D. Activate feature 1 and read every direction: the others pick up part of it. That leak is interference.

if features are dense (often on together), leaks pile up on every input, so the model keeps 2 orthogonal features and drops 3.

as features become sparse, they are rarely on together; the leak is mostly cut by the ReLU, so the model packs all 5 as a pentagon.

more features than dimensions, paid for with interference. One honest clause: superposition makes individual neurons polysemantic only when the neuron basis is privileged, i.e. a nonlinearity acts per neuron, as in an MLP. This toy hidden layer is linear, so here it shows features sharing directions, not neurons as such.

1:13–1:16. Modelo de juguete (Elhage et al. 2022): 5 features, un espacio oculto de 2 dimensiones, y luego reconstruir.

empaqueta 5 direcciones en 2D. Activa la feature 1 y lee todas las direcciones: las demás recogen parte de ella. Esa fuga es la interferencia.

si las features son densas (suelen estar activas a la vez), las fugas se acumulan en cada entrada, así que el modelo se queda con 2 features ortogonales y descarta 3.

a medida que las features se vuelven dispersas, rara vez coinciden; la ReLU corta casi toda la fuga, así que el modelo empaqueta las 5 como un pentágono.

más features que dimensiones, pagado con interferencia. An SAE is a learned dictionary of directions

JumpReLU = a ReLU with a learned threshold per latent (what Gemma Scope 2 uses). Latent labels and values illustrative.

1:16–1:19.

one residual vector, dense, every entry a little bit on.

the encoder maps it to a much wider vector where only a few latents are non-zero (sparsity penalty). Gemma Scope 2 uses JumpReLU: a ReLU with a learned threshold, so a latent is exactly 0 unless its pre-activation clears the threshold. Students will see 'jumprelu' in the NB3 config. Each latent can get a human label from the texts that activate it. Labels here are illustrative.

the decoder adds those latents' directions back up (W_dec·f + b_dec, i.e. Σ latent × direction plus a bias): a reconstruction.

the reconstruction is imperfect. The leftover, the error term, is real computation the dictionary does not explain. Concrete number from NB3: splicing the SAE reconstruction back into Gemma 3 1B IT (layer 17) raises next-token loss by +0.37 nats. Block 4 will draw this leftover as error nodes.

1:16–1:19.

un vector residual, denso, con todas las entradas un poco activas.

el decoder vuelve a sumar las direcciones de esos latentes (W_dec·f + b_dec, es decir, Σ latente × dirección más un sesgo): una reconstrucción.

la reconstrucción es imperfecta. Lo que sobra, el término de error, es cómputo real que el diccionario no explica. Block 3 · Decompose · a caution on steering

A steering effect does not identify the representation

In their experiments, directions orthogonal to a steering vector reached 95–100% of its effect.

So "steering with feature f changes the output" shows f is sufficient to cause the change, not that f is the variable the model uses.

Venkatesh & Kurapath, “On the Non-Identifiability of Steering Vectors in LLMs”, ICLR 2026 workshop (Re-Align), arXiv 2602.06801.

1:38–1:40. Source: Venkatesh & Kurapath, a poster at the ICLR 2026 Representational Alignment workshop, not the main conference. The 95–100% figure is from the paper body (arXiv v3, contributions: "orthogonal perturbations achieving 95–100% of the original steering efficacy across multiple models and traits"); the abstract says "near-equivalent efficacy". Many directions do the same job. Steering is an existence proof of an effect, not evidence of a unique representation. Combine with necessity tests (ablation, patching). This sets up Block 4: we want to validate whole explanations by intervention.

1:38–1:40. Fuente: Venkatesh y Kurapath, un póster del workshop de Representational Alignment de ICLR 2026, no de la conferencia principal.
